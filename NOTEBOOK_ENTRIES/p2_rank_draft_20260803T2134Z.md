**The claim had to be narrowed twice.**

1. **We cannot claim discovery of the negative.** LiDAR has it. The draft says so in the abstract, in
   §1.2 before any of our results, and in §5.1.
2. **RankMe hedges in ways that exempt four of our six instances.** Verbatim: *"a necessary (but not
   sufficient) condition"* (said four times) and *"RankMe should however only be used to compare
   different runs of a given method"*. A high-rank representation with poor performance therefore does

**What survives, and it reorders the paper.** The strongest instance is no longer the dilution sweep.
It is **D2 (§4.3)**: one method (`programme_only` in both arms), one architecture, matched by
construction (`pair_manifest_sha256 ce1352e0…`), one variable changed, in-distribution held-out
evaluation, non-degenerate ranks, three seeds, 2,000-repeat paired patient+cancer bootstraps. That is
RankMe's own reserved regime, and a rank-based selection rule gets it right once in three: seed 42
orders correctly (23.39 vs 14.87), seed 43 **inverts** (28.77 vs **34.12**, PBS higher and still
losing by −0.1089), seed 44 is **tied to two decimals** (9.14 vs 9.11) with a −0.1226 gap. Both CIs
exclude zero 3/3.

Second, and I believe not in the prior art: **a reproducibility floor on the statistic** (§4.7). R1
rank spans **9.11–34.12** across three seeds of one configuration and **8.68 vs 23.39** for one seed

a check a practitioner can run: *retrain one configuration, measure the rank spread, and require the
between-configuration difference to exceed it.* On this stack that would have disqualified every rank

### Scoping recommendation on the liveness-gate and queue-anchoring material: **SPLIT OUT**

The question was whether `paper/LIVENESS_GATE_DESIGN.md` and `paper/QUEUE_ANCHORING.md` should fold
into P2, split out, or be dropped. **Split out, as one short methods paper combining both.** Four
arguments, the third decisive.

1. **Different claim type.** P2's claim is about a *metric* — what a scalar does and does not measure.
   The gate material's claim is about *process* — where a gate must be read from, and that a gate's
   regime must match the run's in every dimension the run's failure can live in. They share a mood
   ("your measurement is not measuring what you think") but not a thesis, and a paper with two theses
   argues neither.
2. **The gate material is already a complete paper.** Four instances with a design rule each, an
   honest-limits section, a falsified-and-withdrawn mechanism (`τ/T`), a fix that works with an open
   mechanism, and a 6/8 bimodal reproducibility finding. It is not an appendix; folding it in would
   force it to be cut.
3. **Decisive: `QUEUE_ANCHORING.md` grades its own central result in effective rank, and nothing
   else.** Its momentum sweep is a table of R3 rank at m ∈ {0, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999}, and the fix is
   declared to work because rank goes 2.23 → 7.42 at step 600. No channel was measured. Putting that
   three pages after "effective rank is uninformative about the channel" hands a referee a free hit.
   It is *reconcilable* — P2 §4.9 establishes that rank near its floor is a valid collapse alarm, and
   the momentum sweep lives entirely in the collapse regime (1.6–9.4 out of 256), which is exactly
   where P2 says rank works — but reconciling it costs a paragraph of hedging inside P2's strongest
   section, in exchange for evidence P2 does not need. Kept separate, each paper's use of rank is
   unambiguous and the gate paper can simply cite P2's §4.9 for its licence.
4. **The overlap is one datum, not a body of evidence, and it is now the weakest one.** Instance 3 is
   the shared item, and this draft demotes it to 4th of six and withdraws its description. The gate
   paper wants it for what it shows about *the gate* (a frozen queue removed the dynamic that causes
   the failure); P2 wants it for what it shows about *hard versus effective rank*. Those are different
   readings of the same table and both papers can carry it without collision, provided P2 states the
   hard-rank caveat and the gate paper does not repeat P2's claim.

**Do not drop it.** The strongest single item in either file is instance 4 of `LIVENESS_GATE_DESIGN.md`
— the gate read from a re-implementation of itself inverted the verdict on 2 of 3 seeds and cost two
launches — and it is now *strengthened* by `g26_is_not_reproducible`, which shows the gate is
non-reproducible run to run (650× spread, 6/8 pass rate, bimodal). That combination — "read the gate
from the process that will train" plus "and a single reading of it is weak evidence either way" — is a
publishable design rule that neither P1 nor P2 has room for.

**One correction the split-out paper must make before it is submitted.** Both files cite MoCo as a
bare "He et al., 2020" with **no arXiv number, DOI or venue anywhere in this repository**, and
`momentum_rescues_rank_but_staleness_is_not_the_mechanism` makes four specific claims about what MoCo
argues, on which a falsification rests. MoCo is now verified: He, Fan, Wu, Xie & Girshick, CVPR 2020,
arXiv:1911.05722. Our characterisation is **correct** — MoCo does argue from key-to-key inconsistency
across the queue (*"its encoded keys are the most outdated and thus the least consistent with the
newest ones"*). Two things must be added: MoCo advances it **twice as a hypothesis** (*"Our hypothesis
is that…"*, *"We hypothesize that such failure is caused by…"*), never as an established mechanism; and
MoCo ties it specifically to queue use (*"a core to making use of a queue"*), so our falsification is
in scope — which should be stated rather than left to be inferred. Verbatim quotes are in
`P2_RANK_DRAFT.md` §2.4.

### In plain terms

Effective rank is a cheap number people compute to check whether a model's internal representation is
any good, without needing labels. We have six cases where it disagrees with the information we can
actually measure. Writing them up properly turned up three things we did not expect.

First, someone has already published the main point. A 2023 paper measured the same rank score against
real downstream performance and found a correlation of 0.32, and one of the original authors of the
rank method has since co-signed a paper saying it does not work. We are a replication in a new field,
not a discovery, and the draft says that in the abstract.

Second, the original rank paper is much more careful than the practice built on it. It says four
separate times that high rank is *necessary but not sufficient*, and it says the score should only be
used to compare runs of the same method. Four of our six cases fall outside those limits, so they do
not actually contradict it. One does — a comparison of two models that are identical except for what
they were asked to predict, three seeds, with error bars — and there the rank score picks the better
model once out of three tries.

Third, and this is the part I think is new: the rank number is not stable enough to do the job even
when it is right. Retrain the identical model with the identical seed on this machine and the rank
changes by a factor of 2.7, while the thing it is supposed to explain barely moves. Any comparison
smaller than that is noise, and every rank comparison this project has made is smaller than that.

Along the way I found that we have three different functions in this codebase all called
`effective_rank`, computing three different things, and that the four historical results were not all
measured with the same one. That is embarrassing and it is also the best illustration of the paper's
point I could have asked for.

I also had to withdraw our most dramatic example. The "rank stayed at maximum while the model
collapsed to a single point" result was measuring a different kind of rank — a plain matrix rank,
whose maximum was fixed at 16 by the batch size — and a measurement in the same folder shows that the
proper effective rank *did* catch that collapse, falling from 12.88 to 1.00. So the honest claim is
narrower: rank tells you when a model has completely collapsed, and nothing else. And even for that, a
simpler number we already compute works better.
