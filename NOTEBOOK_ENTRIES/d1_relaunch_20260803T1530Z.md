## 2026-08-03 15:30 UTC — Correction: the first D1 launch was stopped by its own gate; budget raised to 2400 and relaunched

**Logged:** 2026-08-03 15:30 UTC. **How obtained:** `~/e0_run/d1_v1_launch.log` on the A100
(`150.136.45.194`). Amends the 14:00 entry.

### Technical

The first D1 launch aborted. `programme_free` seed 42 failed its **in-runner** G2.6 at
`contrastive=0.19540` (reduction 0.959, full_consistency 0.00242) — against my standalone harness,
which read **0.01871** for the nominally same seed. The discrepancy is not a bug in either: the
runner builds its model with the full `V2ModelConfig` the experiment uses and reaches the gate with a
different RNG history, so `copy.deepcopy(model)` starts from a different initialisation and different
dropout draws than a freshly constructed model does. **The standalone harness is an approximation of
the gate; the runner's own invocation is the ground truth.** That is worth stating plainly, because
three seeds passing in the harness was taken as sufficient evidence to launch, and it was not.

This is the 800-step marginality recorded in the 14:00 entry, arriving exactly where that entry said
it might. The budget has been raised to 2400 steps in **both** arms and D1 relaunched from a clean run
root.

**What changed and what did not.** The criterion is untouched: ≤ 0.10 contrastive, ≤ 0.02
consistency, ≥ 0.80 reduction. What changed is the compute allowed to demonstrate memorisation.
The justification is that failures at 800 are mid-descent, not stalls, measured four ways:

| configuration | @800 | later |
|---|---|---|
| seed 43, budget 8192, pre-clipping | 0.1766 | 0.0477 @1600 |
| seed 43, budget 8192, clipped | 0.0121 | 0.0047 @2400 |
| seed 42, budget 32768 | 0.6304 | 0.0185 @2400 |
| seed 42, in-runner | 0.1954 | — (the failure that forced this) |

**A longer budget cannot rescue a model that genuinely cannot memorise.** A collapsed biology head
sits at *exactly* ln 16 = 2.7726 and stays there — measured flat from step 2500 through 5000 in the
original sweep, and at 3000 steps in every collapsed arm of this investigation. Extra steps buy a dead
model nothing, so the gate's discriminating power is unchanged. That asymmetry is the whole reason
this is a budget change and not a threshold change.

The contrastive arm now also records its descent trajectory, as the Hallmark arm already did. This
gate has now produced all three failure shapes — dead from step 0, still falling at the last step, and
fell-then-reversed — and a single terminal number cannot tell them apart. Recording the trajectory
means the next person does not have to re-derive which one they are looking at.

### In plain terms

I launched D1 on the strength of a stand-in test that ran the same check in a slightly different
setting, and the real check inside the training job disagreed on one of six runs. The real check was
right to stop the job — that is exactly its purpose, and it cost twelve minutes instead of six hours.

The reason it disagreed is that the check has to memorise sixteen patients within a fixed number of
attempts, and for some starting points that number was just barely too small. Given three times as
many attempts every case measured succeeds comfortably. Crucially, a model that is genuinely broken
does not benefit from more attempts at all — it sits exactly at the "random guessing" score forever —
so lengthening the check does not make it easier to pass while broken.

### Meaning for the claim

No scientific claim is affected; nothing had trained. The methodological point is worth keeping: a
gate re-implemented outside the runner is evidence about the re-implementation. Future G2.6 changes
should be validated by launching the runner and letting it fail closed, which is cheap, rather than
by a harness that reproduces the gate function but not its caller.
